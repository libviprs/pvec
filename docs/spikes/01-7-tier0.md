# Spike P1.7: Tier 0 complete visual coverage

Code: `spikes/tier0/` (throwaway), on the shared fixtures of `00-fixtures.md`, **corpus revision
`pvec-p1-fixtures-r1`**. Every number below is **native x64**: MARS (Intel Core i7-14700F), `rustc
1.99.1`, `--release`, one thread, in a `linux/amd64` container via `tools/spike.sh`. Nothing was run
on arm64. Reproduce with `tools/spike.sh tier0 'cargo run -q --release --bin measure 3'`.

```text
Tier 0 = exact cheap geometry + bounded-error previews for deferred entities
```

**Previews are display fidelity only.** A preview is a bounded rendering approximation under a
screen-space error budget. It is not geometrically or mathematically exact, it is never reported as
such, and the exact entity it names always supersedes it.

## Assumptions

* **Screen and zoom.** The whole drawing extent fits 1920 px across ("fit zoom"), so `scale =
  1920 / extent width` px per world unit: 0.32 for small-synthetic, 0.096 for spline-heavy, 0.0192
  for million, 0.06 for straddler.
* **Error budget.** 0.5 px at fit zoom, i.e. `0.5 / scale` world units: 1.56, 5.21, 26.04 and 8.33
  units. Pixels in every table are `world units * scale`.
* **Deviation metric (named).** *Maximum sagitta*, measured as the two-sided Hausdorff distance
  between the preview and the P1.3 reference evaluator: dense samples of the exact curve (at least
  256, and 16 per initial piece for splines) against the preview polyline, and 7 points per preview
  segment against the polyline through the dense samples. The dense polyline has its own chord error,
  far below any budget here, and I did not subtract it. Previews are stored as `f32` offsets from an
  origin, and the measurement is taken **after** that quantisation.
* **Size model.** The "packed artifact" is the sum of an exact record per entity: id (4) + tag (1) +
  `f64` payload (line 32, circle 24, arc 40, ellipse 56, polyline `4 + 16n`, spline `6 + 16n + 8(n+p+1)`
  plus `8n` if rational, composite the sum of its parts). It is a budgeting model, not the layout P1.5
  will recommend, and the P1.5 sizes were not available when I measured. I also give the ratio to the
  Part 21 baseline of `00-fixtures.md`.
* **Tier 0 exact class.** Lines, circles, arcs and polylines of at most 64 vertices. Everything else
  (ellipse arcs, splines, composites) is deferred and needs a preview. Every entity is first built
  through the P1.3 evaluator, so an entity the evaluator refuses under the numeric policy cannot be
  shipped exact.

The counts reconcile to the total on every fixture: a test (`every_entity_is_exact_previewed_or_listed_with_a_reason`)
checks that each id is in exactly one class, and the measurement asserts the sum.

**Entities in neither class: none, on any fixture.** That was not true at first. My first version
started its adaptive polyline from 8 pieces and judged each piece from three samples, and on
spline-heavy 1,342 splines (up to 60 control points) exceeded the budget, worst 15.06 units against 5.21, and 42
of them had no other form that qualified, so they came out in neither class. Starting from four pieces per knot span fixed
both; the earlier numbers are not used anywhere in this document. The class stays in the design: an
entity the evaluator refuses (a zero-length line, a zero radius) lands in neither with the evaluator's
error, which a test covers, and so does an entity for which no preview form meets the budget within the
4,096-vertex cap.

measured deviation is within the budget. Selection takes the cheapest qualifying form in bytes.
"Built" is how many previews of that form could be made, "within" how many met the budget.

Mean bytes per preview, million: point stub 32, bbox stub 56, control polygon 76.9 (ellipse), 79.8
(spline), 111.8 (composite), sagitta polyline 96.0, 163.8, 175.7. On spline-heavy the control polygon
averages 287.9 bytes per spline and the sagitta polyline 975.6, because a 60-point spline needs a lot
of chords to stay within 5 units.

**Decision, per curve type.**

* **Ellipse arc: sagitta polyline** as the default form. The control polygon is the cheapest form
  that qualifies for most ellipses on million (16,295 of 30,033) but it overshoots the budget by 34.21
  against 26.04 at its worst, so it can only be used where measured. The polyline is the one form that
  meets the budget for 100% of ellipses on all four fixtures.
* **Spline: control polygon where measured within the budget, sagitta polyline otherwise.** The
  control polygon qualified for 28,354 of 29,201 splines on spline-heavy and 100% on million and
  straddler, at 80 to 288 bytes against 164 to 976 for the polyline. Its worst deviation (9.148 against a
  budget of 5.21 on spline-heavy) shows it is **not a bound by construction**: it qualifies only
  because most splines are small against the budget, so the writer must measure it, never assume it.
* **Composite: control polygon where measured, sagitta polyline otherwise.** It qualified for every
  composite on spline-heavy, million and straddler. The polyline is the fallback (10 of 18 on
  small-synthetic, where the budget is tight).
* **Bounding stub and point stub: only for entities small against the budget.** Their worst
  deviations (150 to 850 units) show they are never safe unmeasured; they qualified for 10,823 of
  30,033 ellipses on million, and for 4 splines on spline-heavy.

The error bound a conforming writer must meet is the budget itself: measured two-sided deviation at
most `0.5 px / scale`, after quantisation, checked by the writer against the exact curve for every
preview. For circular arcs the sagitta has a closed form `r (1 - cos(theta / 2))` and the polyline
meets it (a test checks the worst chord against it); for ellipses and splines the adaptive polyline is
verified after the fact, not proved, and I did not derive a curvature bound for rational splines.

| small-synthetic | 1.558 | 0.4987 | 2.01x | 0.162 |

| million | 26.04 | 0.5000 | 2.00x | 0.193 |
| straddler | 8.332 | 0.4999 | 2.00x | 0.167 |

No chosen preview exceeds the budget. Because the rule picks the cheapest qualifying form, the worst
chosen preview sits exactly at the budget on every fixture; that is the selection working, not a
coincidence. At twice the fit zoom the worst preview is 1 px off, which is where the exact entity has
to have replaced it.

## Tier 0 byte budget

| fixture | preview bytes | Tier 0 stream bytes | % of packed artifact | % of Part 21 baseline | packed artifact bytes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| small-synthetic | 11,392 | 64,260 | 99.61% | 20.39% | 64,513 |
| spline-heavy | 9,121,184 | 10,287,202 | 37.03% | 11.46% | 27,777,210 |
| million | 5,881,680 | 58,636,991 | 90.51% | 16.03% | 64,784,879 |
| straddler | 714,632 | 5,982,583 | 92.20% | 16.95% | 6,488,707 |

This is the uncomfortable result. With "all exact cheap geometry" in Tier 0, the first paint is 90 to
99% of the whole packed artifact on three of the four fixtures, because lines and short polylines are
about 90% of the entities and nearly all of the bytes. Tier 0 is a real tier only on spline-heavy,
where the deferred splines are most of the bytes (37%). The 1M-entity drawing needs 58.6 MB for a
complete Tier 0 under this model, which is not an initial-load budget.

I tried the obvious reduction, a **collapse policy**: an exact-class entity whose bounds fit in the
budget is replaced by a point stub if the stub is within the budget (still one record per entity, so
the identity is kept):

| fixture | exact | previewed | Tier 0 stream bytes | % of packed artifact | max deviation (units / px) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| small-synthetic | 916 | 84 | 64,260 | 99.61% | 1.558 / 0.4987 |
| spline-heavy | 19,307 | 30,693 | 10,286,891 | 37.03% | 5.208 / 0.5000 |
| million | 711,959 | 288,041 | 56,427,663 | 87.10% | 26.04 / 0.5000 |
| straddler | 91,625 | 8,375 | 5,977,733 | 92.13% | 8.332 / 0.4999 |

It saves 3.8% of the stream on million (58.64 to 56.43 MB), because a point stub is 32 bytes and the
smallest exact record is 37. Per-entity identity puts a floor of about 32 bytes under every entity, so
**a complete per-entity Tier 0 cannot be small for a million entities**. The lever is not a smaller
stub. It is a spatial and zoom decision: Tier 0 would have to carry a coverage representation that
does not keep one record per entity (aggregate a cell's sub-pixel entities into one stub) and replace
it with exact entities by viewport, which is P1.6 and P5.4 territory. I did not prototype aggregation,
because the acceptance wording needs one preview per deferred entity and the replacement identity
needs one id each. I record the finding for the progressive-tier ADR.

## Time to a complete Tier 0

Median of 3 runs unless noted, single thread, native x64.

| fixture | full selection with verification (ms, 1 run) | ns/entity | generation only (ms) | ns/entity | decode (ms) | ns/entity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| small-synthetic | 48 | 47,908 | 0.2 | 189 | 0.01 | 6 |
| spline-heavy | 684,677 | 13,693,538 | 524.5 | 10,490 | 1.02 | 20 |
| million | 38,424 | 38,424 | 176.6 | 177 | 6.96 | 7 |
| straddler | 3,917 | 39,168 | 17.2 | 172 | 0.67 | 7 |

*Full selection* builds and measures every candidate form of every deferred entity against the exact
curve, which this spike needs and a writer would not do per entity: 684 s on spline-heavy is the cost
of 16 dense samples per initial piece against up to a few hundred polyline segments, per candidate.
*Generation only* is the realistic writer cost: reference curve, adaptive polyline, quantise, no
verification. It leaves out choosing the cheaper form. *Decode* parses the whole Tier 0 stream and
sums every coordinate: 6.96 ms for million, 7 ns per entity.

Read these as a lower bound for the writer and an honest figure for the reader. A production writer
would verify cheaply, not by dense Hausdorff, and I did not design that.

## Replacement identity

A preview carries `replaces`, the file-local `EntityId` of the exact entity that supersedes it. That is
the whole identity: there is no separate preview id, so preview and exact cannot resolve to different
entities. Checks (all tests):

* `check_identity` requires every entity id to be accounted for exactly once across exact, previews and
  neither, rejects a preview naming an id twice and one naming an id that does not exist, and the test
  forges both and sees both caught;
* stability across reassignment: under a second policy that ships ellipse arcs exact, every ellipse
  that was a preview becomes an exact record with the same id, and previews that survive keep the same
  `replaces`. The identity belongs to the entity, not to its tier.

This is the identity P3.6 has to make stable; the spike only shows that a single file-local id is
enough and what must hold.

## Hostile input

The Tier 0 decoder checks every length against the bytes that remain before reading or allocating: the
record counts are rejected if they cannot fit in the stream, a polyline length above 2^20 or a preview
above 4,096 vertices is refused, an unknown tag or form is refused, trailing bytes are refused, and
every truncation of a valid stream is an error. Tests cover truncation at every 37th byte, a lying
count of `u32::MAX`, bad magic, trailing bytes, an absurd polyline length and an unknown tag. There is
no `unsafe` in the spike.

## What I did not measure

* arm64 anything, and any WASM build or run of the Tier 0 reader.
* Preview rendering cost, GPU buffers, or how a map actually looks. Error is geometric, in pixels at a
  stated zoom, never a visual judgement.
* A different budget. 0.5 px at fit zoom is the only budget I ran; the tradeoff curve is not measured.
* Aggregation of sub-pixel entities into shared stubs (see above), and any spatial tiling of Tier 0.
* A cheap, provable verification for rational splines. The sagitta polyline is verified after the
  fact against dense samples, not bounded by derivation, so the measured figure could miss a narrow
  excursion between samples.
* Sizes in the layout P1.5 recommends. All "packed" figures are the model above.
* Fixtures with real-world coordinate magnitude or rotated, non-axis-aligned content; the fixtures are
  plan-like with rounded coordinates.
